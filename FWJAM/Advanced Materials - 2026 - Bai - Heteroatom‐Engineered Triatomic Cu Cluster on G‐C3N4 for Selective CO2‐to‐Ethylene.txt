1Introduction
Electrochemical conversion of carbon dioxide (CO2) into value-
added chemicals and fuels offers a practical route for carbon
recycling and a promising strategy to decouple chemical pro-
duction from fossil feedstocks [ 1]. By transforming CO2into
products such as CO, formic acid (HCOOH), methane (CH4),
ethylene (C2H4), and ethanol (CH3CH2OH), electrochemical
CO2reduction (CO2RR) can both mitigate emissions and store
renewableelectricityinchemicalbonds,advancingeffortstoward
carbonneutrality[ 2,3].Single-atom catalysts (SACs) have drawn intense attention for
CO2RRbecausetheyachievenearlycompleteatomicutilization
and allow precise control of local coordination environments
[4, 5]. Anchoring isolated metal atoms (for example, Fe, Co,
Ni, or Cu) on N-rich supports has delivered breakthroughs in
activity and selectivity for two-electron and other C1products.
Nevertheless,SACsfaceintrinsiclimitationsfortwo-carbon(C2)
productformation:atomicdispersionsuppressesthepresenceof
adjacent active sitesrequired for productive C ─C coupling, and
isolatedatomsaresusceptibletomigrationandaggregationunder
reducingconditions,compromisingstability[ 6,7].

2.3EnergyBarrierAnalysisofC ─CCouplingon
Heteroatom-DopedCuClusters
The electrochemical reduction of CO2to C2products critically
dependsontheefficientformationofC ─Cbonds[34],whichis
governedbythestabilizationandcouplingofkeyintermediates
suchas*CO,*COH, and *CHO.Understandingthekineticand

focusing on the energy barriers associated with the coupling
of key intermediates ( *CO,*COH,*CHO) and identifying the
most preferable reaction pathways. We examined three primary
coupling pathways on Cu3clusters: (i) direct *CO dimerization
(*CO–CO),(ii)couplingbetween *CO and hydrogenated *CHO
(*CO–CHO),and(iii)couplingbetween *CO and hydrogenated
*COH (*CO–COH). *CHO and *COH form by protonation of
*CO at the oxygen and carbon atoms, respectively, yielding
intermediateswithdistinctadsorptiongeometriesandreactivity
(Figure3a). Since C ─C coupling generally exhibits the highest
energybarrieramongallelementarystepsinC2productforma-

[35].Transitionstatetheorywasappliedtoevaluatetheenergybarriers

hydrogenationof *COto*COHislessadvantageous.Therefore,

mechanism. In the Tafel mechanism, *CO is protonated at the
oxygen end, forming *CHO, while the Heyrovsky mechanism

3Conclusions
In summary, we systematically investigate the catalytic perfor-
mance of heteroatom-doped Cu3clusters supported on g-C3N4
for the selective electrochemical reduction of CO2to C2H4,
leveragingtheirinherentadvantagesinC ─Ccouplingreactions.
ByselectivelymodifyingCuclusterswithnon-metaldopants(B,
O, S, P, and Se), we identify seven thermodynamically stable
configurations and analyse their CO2RR pathways and compet-
ing HER. Transition-state calculations reveal that the *CO+
*CHO→*COCHOcouplingstepistherate-determiningprocess,
with P- and Se-doped clusters (Cu3-C3N2P2-3 and Cu3-C3N2Se2-
1) exhibiting significantly reduced energy barriers of 0.84 and
0.92 eV, respectively. Thermodynamic analysis further demon-
stratesastrongpreferenceforC2H4formationoverCH3CH2OH,
with energy barriers as low as 0.33 and 0.10 V for the P-
andSe-dopedsystems,respectively.Electronicstructureanalysis
reveals that first-shell substitution with P or Se creates charge-
asymmetric sites that enhance *CO and*COCHO adsorption,
stabilizekeyintermediates,andshiftantibondingCu ─COstates
to higher energies, thereby promoting efficient C ─C coupling.
Electrochemical evaluations demonstrated that the Se-modified
catalystdeliverssuperiorC2+selectivity,achievingaremarkable
ethyleneFaradaicefficiencyof ∼54%atahighcurrentdensityof
250mAcm−2,stableoperationover30hunderthetestedflow-cell
conditions.Collectively,theseresultsunderscorethecriticalrole
ofmaintainingshortCu ─Cuseparationsandengineeringcharge-
asymmetric sites via heteroatom doping in optimizing C ─C
couplingkinetics.Thisworkprovidesamechanisticframework
andpracticaldesignprinciplesfordevelopinghigh-performance
catalysts, paving the way for sustainable carbon recycling tech-
nologies.
