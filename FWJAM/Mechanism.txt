The research and development of efficient and environmentally friendly dust suppression technology has become 
an important direction for clean utilization of coal. Traditional dust suppressants rely on chemical surfactants, 
while environmental protection dust suppressants based on natural biological materials have become a research 
hotspot because of their biodegradability, high wettability, and low ecological toxicity. This study aims to sys-
tematically investigate the mechanism by which the natural polyphenolic compound tannic acid affects the 
wettability of bituminous coal through an integrated approach combining molecular dynamics simulations and 
experimental methods. Firstly, the microscopic interaction mechanisms underlying tannic acid’s enhancement of 
aqueous solution spreading and adsorption on coal surfaces was examined through analysis of simulation pa-
rameters including hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions. Then, the wetting parameters, including 
surface tension, contact angle, and water retention rate, were measured by experiments to study the wetting 
properties of tannic acid and verify the accuracy of molecular dynamics simulation results. Molecular dynamics 
simulations reveal that tannic acid increases the water diffusion coefficient by 24.4 % and the coal-water 
interface overlap area by 155 %. Experiments show that tannic acid reduces the coal-water contact angle 
from 73.2•to 31.4•and the surface tension from 73.32 mN/m to 31.37 mN/m. Tannic acid effectively reduces 
coal-water interfacial energy and enhances wettability. This study provides the theoretical basis and technical 
reference for developing natural biological dust suppressants, which significantly reduce coal mine dust con-
centration, improve the working environment, and reduce secondary pollution.
1.Introduction
China is one of the largest coal producers in the world. Although 
China has made great efforts to develop clean energy in recent years, 
coal is still the main source of energy consumption in China. Carbon 
emissions and environmental pollution caused by coal consumption are 
still the main challenges, and promoting clean coal utilization is an 
important research direction [1–3]. Coal dust has always been an 
important problem affecting mine safety production and can easily 
cause various hazards [4]. It not only posed a serious threat to workers ’ 
health but also polluted the environment and negatively impacted the 
surrounding ecosystem. Long-term exposure to coal dust will signifi -
cantly increase the risk of serious lung diseases. When coal dust is suspended in the air with sufficient concentration and meets a fire 
source, it will cause an explosion and lead to catastrophic accidents 
[5–7]. Therefore, coal enterprises must rationally develop and adopt 
clean production technology, reduce dust hazards, reduce environ -
mental damage, and ensure workers ’ lives and health.
Wettability is one of the key indexes used to evaluate the effective -
ness of dust suppressants. It directly affects the coverage uniformity and 
wetting layer stability of dust suppressants on coal surfaces and then 
determines the dust suppression effect [8–10]. Traditional dust sup-
pressants mainly improve the wettability of coal by adding chemical 
surfactants. However, with the improvement of social and environ -
mental awareness and the deepening of sustainable development, the 
direction of dust suppressants ’ research and development has gradually

Their development and application will promote the development of

At present, a large number of scholars have shifted their research 
objectives to the preparation of environmentally-friendly dust suppres -
sants, mainly from the perspective of biodegradability, considering the 
natural biological materials with strong wetting ability, good moisture 
retention, and bonding effect of coal dust, and combining the evaluation 
indexes such as film-forming performance, etc., to prepare 
environmentally-friendly dust suppressants [14–17]. MIGUEL et al.[18]
used glycerol byproducts generated from biodiesel production as raw 
materials to formulate a non-toxic, pollution-free, high-efficiency dust 
suppressant. Li et al.[19] extracted cellulose from waste paper to syn-

and employed Materials Studio software for molecular dynamics simu-
lations, revealing the micro-interaction mechanism between the dust 
suppressant and coal dust. This dust suppressant is capable of natural 
degradation and does not cause secondary environmental pollution. 
Zhang et al.[20] applied a chemical modification method using natural 
Guar Gum (GG) to prepare a new polymer spray dust suppressant, 
exhibiting substantial wetting and aggregation properties. Lyu et al.[21]
studied the effect of the natural surfactant soybean lecithin on the 
wettability of high-rank coal. The research found that soybean lecithin 
can significantly reduce the gas-liquid surface tension and increase the 
wettability of the coal surface, making coal powder easier to disperse. 
Zhao et al.[22] prepared a new soapberry saponin-based composite coal 
dust suppressant, which exhibitted better wettability than traditional 
chemical surfactants while reducing the amount of chemical surfactant 
needed. This provides a new approach for utilizing soapberry pericarp 
and reduces the economic cost of coal dust suppressants. Jiang et al.[23]
selected four types of biosurfactants with different structural properties. 
They tested their spreading coefficients, adhesion work, adhesion ten-
sion, and other wetting adsorption parameters on the coal surface. They 
concluded that lipopeptides have better wettability and adsorption 
performance on coal than the other three biosurfactants. To sum up, 
many scholars at home and abroad have used microbial products and 
components extracted from plants as biological reagents and applied 
them to dust suppression.
Tannic acid (TA) is a polyphenolic compound in many natural me-
dicinal plants. It has strong biological and pharmacological activities 
and is a natural product with important development value. Its molec -
ular formula is C76H52O46. Tannic acid contains many hydroxyl groups, 
which can easily combine with water molecules and have strong hy-
drophilicity. In addition, tannic acid has excellent oxidation resistance, 
antibacterial activity, and biodegradability. It can interact with various 
organic, inorganic, hydrophilic, and hydrophobic materials through 
hydrogen bonding, static electricity, coordination bonds, and hydro -
phobic interactions [24,25] . Therefore, tannic acid can be used to 
improve the wettability of an aqueous solution on a coal surface. 
Although the existing literature on this subject is limited, considering 
the richness of the remaining natural compounds and derivatives, the 
potential application of a hydrophilic coal surface modified by bio-based 
adsorbents is huge.
Based on previous studies, this paper systematically explores the 
wetting regulation mechanism of tannic acid solution on bituminous 
coal. Firstly, the adsorption behavior and interaction nature of TA at the 
coal-water interface were analyzed by molecular dynamics simulation, 
and the reliability of the simulation results was verified by combining 
macro performance parameters such as surface tension, contact angle, 
and water retention rate. Finally, the correlation between micro- 
interaction and macro-wetting performance was analyzed, which pro-
vides a theoretical basis for developing new bio-based dust suppressants. The research content provides a technical scheme in line with the 
concept of sustainable development for constructing "green mines".
2.Materials and methods
2.1. Molecular dynamics simulation
2.1.1. Construction of molecular model
The Fuchs model was selected as the bituminous coal model, and the 
Forcite module of Materials Studio 2020 was used for geometric opti-
mization and annealing treatment of coal molecules [26,27] . The initial 
configuration of tannic acid (CID:16129778, C76H52O46) was obtained 
from the PubChem database, and the density functional theory (DFT) 
was optimized using the DMol3 module of Materials Studio. The pa-
rameters were selected as GGA-PBE, basis set: DNP 3.5, and convergence 
criterion: energy D1 ×10 5 Ha [28,29] . The molecular structure 
optimized by DFT is introduced into the Forcite module of Materials 
Studio as the initial configuration of MD. The initial energy configura -
tions of raw coal and TA molecular models are shown in Fig. 1, 
respectively.
2.1.2. Simulation system setup
The molecular simulations were systematically performed as follows: 
Using the Amorphous Cell module, we first constructed the initial 
simulation systems by randomly distributing 8 coal molecules within a 
periodic boundary cell. After geometry optimization using the COM -
PASSII forcefield, this yielded a stable coal model containing 3 mole-
cules with cubic dimensions of 23.31 Å and a density of 1.24 g/cm³ , 
consistent with experimental values for bituminous coal. Following the 
same protocol, we subsequently built additional simulation cells con-
taining 2000 water molecules and 1–4 TA molecules respectively. All 
initial structures underwent thorough structural optimization followed 
by an annealing procedure with carefully controlled parameters: a 
heating rate of 10K/ps from 300K to 600K, 200,000 annealing steps, 
and subsequent equilibration through 100ps NVT and 500ps NPT 
simulations at 300K and 1atm, with system convergence confirmed by 
energy fluctuations below 0.5% during the final 200ps. Using the Build 
Layer tool, we then assembled these components into two types of 
interfacial systems-coal/water and coal/TA/water - which were cate-
gorized into five distinct systems (0TA to 4TA) based on TA content as 
shown in Fig. 2. Prior to production runs, all systems were subjected to 
50,000 ps of geometry optimization to eliminate steric conflicts and 
minimize potential energy, with coal molecules position-constrained (a 
treatment verified to have negligible impact on results). The molecular 
dynamics simulations were then conducted using a three-stage protocol: 
initial pre-equilibration under NVT ensemble at 298K using Velocity 
Scale thermostat (1.0 fs timestep), followed by production runs 
employing Nose thermostat for 200ps with 1 fs timestep to ensure 
proper sampling of system configurations [30]. In the simulation pro-
cess, the Ewald algorithm calculated the electrostatic potential inter-
action. The Atom-based algorithm calculated the van der Waals force, 
and the calculated cutoff distance was 12.5 [31]. Finally, the adsorption 
configuration, interaction energy, number of hydrogen bonds, and 
diffusion coefficient of water molecules of the equilibrium system ob-
tained by molecular dynamics simulation were analyzed to explore the 
regulation effect of TA on coal molecular wettability.

To systematically evaluate the wettability of tannic acid (TA) solu-
tion and verify the results of molecular dynamics simulation, a multi- 
parameter synergistic wetting characterization experiment, including 
surface tension, contact angle, and water retention, was designed.

3.Results and discussion
3.1. Analysis of wetting regulation mechanism
3.1.1. Structural characteristics of simulated system before and after 
adsorption of TA
To study the regulation mechanism of TA on coal wettability from 
the microscopic point of view, Materials Studio simulated and analyzed 
the system ’s concentration distribution and adsorption configuration 
before and after the adsorption of different concentrations of TA. The 
equilibrium configuration of bituminous coal-water and bituminous 
coal-TA-water systems was obtained by MS molecular dynamics simu-
lation software, as shown in Fig. 4. Figures A, B, C, D, and E showed the 
simulation results of bituminous coal-water and bituminous coal-tannic 
acid-water systems with 0TA, 1TA, 2TA, 3TA, and 4TA, respectively. It 
can be observed from the figure that the diffusion degree of water 
molecules on the coal surface was significantly higher than that of the 
single water molecule dynamics simulation system after adding TA, 
indicating that the introduction of TA promotes the dispersion of water 
molecules on the coal dust surface, thus enhancing the wetting range of 
water on bituminous coal. From the initial configuration, TA molecules 
were horizontally arranged above the surface of coal molecules, and 
there was a considerable distance between them. After a period of 
interaction, the distance between tannic acid molecules and coal mol-
ecules had changed significantly. In this process, tannic acid molecules 
were gradually adsorbed on the surface of bituminous coal through 
hydrogen bonding, and a stable TA adsorption layer was constructed. 
The abundant phenolic hydroxyl (-OH) groups in the adsorption layer 
provided additional water molecule binding sites for bituminous coal, 
promoting the adsorption of water molecules and enhancing bituminous 
coal’s hydrophilicity. Moreover, with the increase of TA concentration, the adsorption mode changed from isolated distribution to single-layer 
covering and double-layer stacking, decreasing hydrophilicity. For 
this, this paper would further analyze the interaction energy, hydrogen 
bonding, mean square displacement, and diffusion coefficient of water 
molecules under different concentrations and the relative concentration 
distribution of different concentration systems and reveal the wetting 
mechanism of tannic acid to coal from the microscopic level.
3.1.2. Interaction energy analysis
Interaction energy is an important index for evaluating the strength 
of intermolecular interaction in a system. It is defined as the difference 
between the total energy of the system and the sum of the energy of each 
monomer and can be used to reveal the system ’s stability and interaction 
mechanism from the perspective of energy [35]. In the study of wetta -
bility, the influence of surfactants can be explained by the change of 
interaction energy: when coal molecules, surfactant molecules, and 
water molecules reach three-phase adsorption equilibrium, the system 
tends to be stable due to energy release. The more energy released, the 
lower the system ’s total energy, the more stable the adsorption struc-
ture, and the more obvious the wetting effect [36].
In order to explore the intensity of interaction energy between the 
coal surface and tannic acid (TA) solution interface, the Forcite module 
of Materials Studio was used to calculate the interaction energy of water- 
coal and water-TA-coal interface models, respectively. As shown in 
Table 1, the interaction energy of the system varies significantly under 
different concentrations of TA surfactant. Van der Waals, Electrostatic, 
and System interaction energy of the system can be obtained from the

overlapping area of the water-coal interface by 155%, and improving 
the wetting effect of water molecules on the surface of bituminous coal.
3.2. Characterization of macroscopic wetting parameters
3.2.1. Surface tension
There was repulsive force and attractive force between solution and 
dust. The primary function of surfactant was to improve the wetting and 
diffusion ability of water molecules on the solid surface by reducing the 
surface tension of the solution so that the attractive force was greater 
than the repulsive force. The system ’s free energy was reduced in this 
process, and a better wetting effect was achieved. The smaller the surface tension of the solution, the stronger its wettability to coal sam-
ples [41,42] . To further verify the accuracy of molecular dynamics 
simulation results and determine the wetting effect of tannic acid sur-
factants with different concentrations on coal, the surface tension of six 
groups of tannic acid solutions with different concentrations was 
measured, as shown in Fig. 9. The experimental results indicate that the 
surface tension of pure water was 73.32 mN/m, and the high surface 
tension suggests that the interaction between water molecules and the 
bituminous coal surface was weak, making it difficult for water to spread 
and achieve adequate wetting on the coal surface. However, with the 
addition of tannic acid, the surface tension of water decreased signifi -
cantly, which showed that the adsorption of tannic acid molecules on 
the surface of the aqueous solution could effectively reduce the surface 
tension, and this reduction was closely related to the concentration of 
tannic acid. This relationship can be attributed to multiple hydroxyl 
groups in tannic acid molecules, which can form hydrogen bonds with 
water molecules, thus changing the molecular arrangement and inter-
action mode on the solution ’s surface. Under low concentrations, TA 
molecules covered the coal surface in a monolayer adsorption mode, and 
its phenolic hydroxyl groups formed a dense hydrogen bond network 
structure with water molecules. When the concentration of TA was 
0.06 %, the surface tension reached the minimum value of 31.37 mN/m. 
However, when the concentration exceeded a certain value, excessive 
tannic acid molecules formed an aggregated structure in the solution, 
leading to the rising surface tension. This was consistent with the results 
of molecular dynamics simulation of adsorption configuration.

attractive force between water molecules and bituminous coal mole-

better the wetting effect of the solution was. On the contrary, it indicates

surface tension reaches the lowest value at 0.06 %TA, 57.2 % lower than

than 0.06 %, and the molecular aggregation state changed. Corre -

4.Conclusion
This paper systematically studied the influence mechanism of tannic 
acid as a bio-based dust suppressant on the wettability of bituminous 
coal by combining molecular dynamics simulation with macro- 
experiment. The adsorption behavior of tannic acid molecules at the 
coal-water interface and its microscopic mechanism on wettability were 
revealed, and experiments verified the reliability of the simulation re-
sults. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The electrostatic interaction between systems dominates the 
wetting regulation mechanism. The molecular dynamics simula -
tion results show that electrostatic interaction plays a leading role 
in the tannic acid-coal-water system. The phenolic hydroxyl 
group of tannic acid combines with the negatively charged area 
on the coal surface through electrostatic attraction. It forms a 
stable three-phase adsorption layer on the water molecule layer, which significantly reduces the system ’s total energy to 
 3586.58 kcal/mol and increases the overlapping area of the 
coal-water interface, thus improving the wetting efficiency.
(2) Tannic acid can significantly enhance the wettability of coal. The 
experimental and simulation results show that tannic acid forms a 
strong hydrogen bond network with oxygen-containing func-
tional groups on the coal surface through its polyphenol hydroxyl 
structure, which significantly improves the wettability of coal. 
The contact angle decreased from 73.2 to 31.4 in pure water, the 
surface tension decreased from 73.32 mN/m to 31.37 mN/m, and 
the diffusion coefficient of water molecules increased by 24.4 %, 
indicating that tannic acid optimized the interface energy and 
enhanced the migration ability of water molecules.
(3) Tannic acid exhibits a concentration-dependent effect on coal 
wettability. When the concentration is lower than 0.06 %, the 
interfacial energy of tannic acid molecules is reduced by mono -
layer adsorption. After this concentration is exceeded, excessive 
tannic acid molecules form a micelle structure, which leads to the 
recovery of surface tension and the decrease of water retention 
rate. This phenomenon is consistent with the molecular dynamics 
simulation results that tannic acid molecular agglomeration in-
hibits the diffusion of water molecules.
(4) Pearson correlation analysis shows that molecular dynamics 
simulation results are significantly correlated with macro wetting 
parameters,including contact angle, surface tension, water 
retention rate, etc, and the absolute correlation coefficient value 
is 0.78 –1.00. Microscopic parameters such as interaction energy, 
number of hydrogen bonds, and diffusion coefficient of water 
molecules are highly consistent with the changing trend of 
macro-wettability, which verifies the accuracy of simulation re-
sults and the reliability of experimental data.
